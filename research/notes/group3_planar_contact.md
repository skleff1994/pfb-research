## 1. Closest prior art to the M0 results

### 1.1 Peshkin & Sanderson 1988 (FULL, threat HIGH for Thm 4.3 and for the disk case of Thm 4.5)
- "The motion of a pushed, sliding workpiece", IEEE J. Robotics and Automation 4(6):569-598, Dec 1988. Full text: https://peshkin.mech.northwestern.edu/publications/1988_Peshkin_MotionPushedSlidingWorkpiece.pdf (the PDF title page says "IEEE Transactions on Robotics and Automation"). Abstract page: https://ri.cmu.edu/?p=15441. Precursor reports CMU-RI-TR-85-18 (zero pusher friction) and CMU-RI-TR-86-07 (contact friction).
- Problem: a workpiece is pushed at an edge and slides on a support whose pressure distribution is unknown. They find the locus of centers of rotation (COR) over ALL admissible pressure distributions.
- Information assumed (sec. 2.5): P >= 0, supported inside a disk of radius a centered at the CM, total load fixed (= Mg), and first moment zero, i.e. the centroid of the pressure is the CM. That is exactly M0 (mass and CoP known), with the patch relaxed to the circumscribing disk. They state that any distribution on a subset is a distribution on the disk, so the disk locus bounds the locus for the real workpiece.
- Quantities (eqs. 10-11): d_r = ∫P(w)|w-r| dw ("weighted distance from the COR to the pressure distribution") and v_r = ∫P(w)(w-r)/|w-r| dw. The Coulomb dissipation at COR r is mu*d_r, which is the manuscript's h(q) = mu|b|∫||x-p|| dν_N. Their quotient moment is q_r = v_r/d_r.
- Extremal results (sec. 4.5), CM at origin, COR at distance r:
  - min d_r = r, attained when all pressure is at the CM. This is Jensen's inequality and matches Thm 4.3 (lower bound mu f_N ||a||, attained by f_N delta_c). They do not phrase it as a guaranteed torsion capacity of zero, and they do not use a support function.
  - max d_r = sqrt(r^2 + a^2) for the disk, attained by a dipod (two points at opposite ends of a diameter, weights from the centroid constraint). This is the disk case of Thm 4.5.
  - v_r is maximal for pressure at the CM, and minimal for a dipod (r > a) or a dipod giving v_r pointing away from the CM (r < a). Weights for the second dipod are P1 = r/(r+a), P2 = a/(r+a).
- Method: they use first-order optimality of minimum dissipation (grad E_r = 0, eq. 9) and numerical search over tripods. Mason's theorem that three support points suffice is cited, and 4-point supports gave identical loci. Boundary points of the COR locus come from dipods analytically. A "tip" formula r_tip = a^2/(alpha.c) bounds the slowest rotation.
- Guarantee: an outer bound (conservative), since the circumscribing disk is used, not the true support polygon.
- Overlap with the manuscript: Thm 4.3 (Jensen lower bound) and a special case of Thm 4.5 (extremals on at most 2 points for a disk, 3 for a general patch). Their kinematic bound is on COR location. They do not state a bound on the wrench set, a convex or LP reduction for polygons, or taxel data.
- Not in Peshkin-Sanderson: general convex patches, vertex LP, the 3-atom Caratheodory bound, non-identifiability (Cor 4.8), and anything about taxels.

### 1.2 Huang, Bhatia, Boots, Mason, "Exact Bounds on the Contact Driven Motion of a Sliding Object, With Applications to Robotic Pulling" (FULL via local PDF, threat HIGH for Thm 4.5)
- PDF: https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~bboots/files/Huang_pulling.pdf. The reference list gives "Manuscript in submission, 2017", so the final venue is unverified here. Other listings: https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10046030, https://ri.cmu.edu/?p=105879. Verify the venue before citing.
- Problem: a planar slider is pushed or pulled at a sticking contact point, with known support region R and known center of pressure but unknown pressure distribution. They compute exact bounds on angular velocity.
- Math structure (sec. III-B, "frictional moment envelope", attributed to Mason's book 2001):
  - For twist v+ and unit normalized pressure at x, the maximal potential moment is g(x) = -mu f0 x × A(x)v+/||A(x)v+||. G(x) = (x, y, g(x)) is a surface over R.
  - The set {∫G p̂ dA : ∫p̂ = 1} is the convex hull of G(R). Feasible (CoP, moment) pairs are exactly the convex hull. An angular velocity is feasible iff (x0, y0, 0) lies in the hull.
  - This is the same moment-set structure as the manuscript's M0 upper bound: sup of ∫g dν over measures with fixed mass and mean equals the upper concave envelope of g at c, with extremal measures on at most 3 atoms (Caratheodory in R^3). Their moment envelope is the convex hull in (x, y, moment) space and implicitly gives that fact, but they state no atom count or vertex LP.
  - Their Thm 2: the set of feasible angular velocities is connected (convexity of the class of distributions with a given CoP). Algorithm: bisection plus feasibility test.
- Related-work paragraph (quoted statement of prior art): Alexander-Maddocks bound feasible rotation centers within the minimum object-enclosing strip perpendicular to the pusher wrench. Peshkin-Sanderson use the minimum enclosing disk centered at the CoP. A "bisector bound" is in Mason 2001. "In all prior work, the bounds are conservative but not exact." Their claim: first exact bounds over all distributions with shared CoP.
- Quantity bounded: kinematic (angular velocity, rotation center under a point-contact constraint), not the wrench set W(ν_N) about the CoP, and not capacity.
- Overlap: Thm 4.5 in spirit, since the moment envelope is the convex hull used for the sup. The manuscript's novelty relative to this paper is the set W and its support function for general (a, b), the closed-form M0 bounds and the vertex LP, zero guaranteed torsion, non-identifiability, and the taxel results. A de-risking sentence is needed: Huang et al. already use extremal (hull) arguments for unknown pressure with known CoP.

### 1.3 Alexander & Maddocks 1993 (ABS, threat HIGH for the extremal-structure claim)
- "Bounds on the Friction-Dominated Motion of a Pushed Object", Int. J. Robotics Research 12(3):231-248, June 1993. DOI 10.1177/027836499301200303. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/027836499301200303. Full text not accessible (publisher returned 403).
- From the abstract and citing papers: they bound all motions consistent with every friction (pressure) distribution. Main result: for contact regions with one or two connected components, the set of possible motions equals the set generated by friction restricted to TWO-POINT contacts on the boundary of the region. Earlier formulations had only reduced general support to tripods, "which lack an analytic solution". They use a dissipation-function formulation (principle of minimal dissipation) and a numerical scheme.
- Citing papers describe their bound as: rotation centers lie within the minimum object-enclosing strip perpendicular to the pusher wrench (Huang et al.), and as a "purely analytical approach where only the geometric extent of the support area is known" (2020 survey in Frontiers in Robotics and AI, https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2020.00008/full, not read in full).
- Inferred structure (not read from the paper): extremize the dissipation or wrench over measures supported on the region, with support-region knowledge only (no CoP constraint is clearly assumed); extremals are boundary dipods. I could not confirm whether CoP/mass is also constrained. The 2-point statement is for the motion set, and is stated for regions with one or two components (convexity not required).
- Overlap: direct precedent for "extremal distributions are concentrated on few boundary points". The manuscript's claim of 3 atoms (and vertices for polygons) under a fixed CoP is a different constraint set (fixed mean), so the count 3 versus 2 is consistent with the moment problem (an extra linear constraint adds one atom). They should be cited and compared. Must read the paper before finalizing the novelty claim.

### 1.4 Mason: voting theorem and moment envelope (CIT/ABS, threat MEDIUM)
- M. T. Mason, "Mechanics and planning of manipulator pushing operations", IJRR 5(3), 1986. I could not retrieve the SAGE record; volume and pages are from memory and must be verified. Huang et al. cite "M. T. Mason, On the scope of quasi-static pushing, ISRR 1986" and the 2001 book "Mechanics of Robotic Manipulation" (MIT Press, ISBN 0-262-13396-2) for the bisector bound and the moment envelope (Thm 7.4 of the book).
- Result: rotation direction (clockwise/counterclockwise) of a pushed object is independent of the pressure distribution (Peshkin-Sanderson cite "Mason's theorem 5": three support points suffice). Overlap with M0: a qualitative sign result for all distributions, and a three-point reduction. Verify the exact theorem numbering.

## 3. Simulators
- MuJoCo (documentation, https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/index.html): condim 4 adds torsional friction, whose coefficient has "units of length which can be interpreted as the diameter of the surface contact patch". Condim 6 adds rolling friction. A scalar effective radius; no pressure distribution, no bounds. Threat none.
- Elandt, Drumwright, Sherman, Ruina, "A pressure field model for fast, robust approximation of net contact force and moment between nominally rigid objects", IROS 2019, arXiv:1904.11433 (ABS): pressure fields yield a contact surface, tractions and net wrench, with friction integrated over the patch, so torsional friction arises from the computed pressure. Pressure is computed, not bounded. Threat none (a source of pressure fields for simulation).
- Masterjohn, Guoy, Shepherd, Castro, "Velocity level approximation of pressure field contact patches", IEEE RA-L 7(4):11593-11600, 2022, arXiv:2110.04157 (ABS): discrete-time velocity-level hydroelastic contact in Drake. I did not confirm how torsional friction is handled. Threat none.
- Hydrosoft (arXiv:2509.13126) mentions non-holonomic hydroelastic models for tactile manipulation with circular patches (ABS from search snippet only); Tac2Motion (arXiv:2509.17812) uses a heuristic virtual torque scaled by tactile readings. Relevant to taxel-based torsion proxies; threat none to low.

## 4. Worst-case torsional capacity and CoP/torque relation
- No paper found that states a guaranteed (inf) torsional capacity from M0 data, or a taxel-based guaranteed torsion bound. The closest: Peshkin-Sanderson d_r minimum (distance of COR to CM) and the 6D contact work of Bouchard et al. (McGill, https://www.cs.mcgill.ca/~kry/pubs/6d/6dContact.pdf, ABS): torque limits from the contact patch and CoP location in rigid-body simulation (threat low).
- The "maximal torque when CoP near the patch edge" claim: under the manuscript's M0 upper bound, the max torque about the CoP is attained by a polygon-vertex measure. Peshkin-Sanderson's dipod analysis (max d_r when pressure is at two opposite disk points) is the same phenomenon.
- Unpublished: Y. de Mont-Marin, "Contact patch" draft (Prop 4.4), the manuscript says the M0 upper bound coincides with it. Search found no published version (could not verify).

## 5. Novelty assessment for the manuscript
1. Thm 4.3 (Jensen lower bound, zero guaranteed torsion): Peshkin-Sanderson (min d_r = r at the CM) contain the same inequality for the Coulomb dissipation at a COR. Cite and frame as "capacity version"; novelty is the wrench-set (support function) formulation and the explicit guarantee statement. Risk: medium-high.
2. Thm 4.5 / Cor 4.6 (sup over measures with fixed mass and mean, at most 3 atoms, vertex LP): conceptually preceded by Peshkin-Sanderson (dipods on a disk), Alexander-Maddocks (two-point boundary reduction, tripods before), and Huang et al. (convex hull moment envelope for fixed CoP). The measure-theoretic statement with Radon measures, Caratheodory atom count, Edmundson-Madansky vertex LP for general convex polygons, and application to the capacity set W are not found in the sources I could access. Risk: medium-high; must be positioned as a generalization, not a first.
3. Cor 4.8 (non-identifiability unless c is extreme): no precedent found. Risk low.
4. Taxel-based results (M_F, M_T, Thms 6.1-7.3): no precedent found in this group. Risk low.
